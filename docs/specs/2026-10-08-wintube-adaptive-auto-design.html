WinTube Adaptive Auto

Auto quality picks what the connection can actually carry, and steps down when playback stalls. Two levels: a rate-informed pick at video start, and a buffering-triggered downshift mid-play — both reusing the reload-at-position machinery, with the measured rate visible in the quality menu and a simulation key for testing.

Base WinTube v2.9.0 Constraint mpv plays one HLS variant; continuous ABR stays out of scope

1 · Rate measurement

  • demuxer-cache-state already carries raw-input-rate (bytes/s). DemuxerCacheState parses it alongside the ranges; MpvPlayerHost exposes a smoothed rate (exponential moving average over ~5 s of the 1 s cache polls, ignoring zero samples while paused/idle).
  • The latest smoothed rate is persisted to settings.json (measuredBandwidthBps) on player teardown, seeding the NEXT session's first pick. No stored rate and no measurement yet → Auto behaves exactly as today (screen height only).
  • Test override: optional simulatedBandwidthMbps in settings.json replaces the measured rate everywhere (pick, display, persistence is skipped while simulating).

2 · The Auto pick (level 1)

  • Pure Core function: highest rung whose height ≤ the monitor height (unchanged from today) AND whose BANDWIDTH (bits/s) ≤ rate × 8 × 0.7; the rate can only pull the choice DOWN from the screen-based rung, never above it. No usable rate → screen height only. The 0.7 safety factor and all other tunables are named constants in one place.
  • Applied where Auto resolves today: video start and every quality reload. The label stays "Auto · 1080p".

3 · Stall downshift (level 2)

  • MpvPlayerHost observes mpv's paused-for-cache flag and raises a buffering event on each false→true edge (marshalled like the other events).
  • PlayerPage, only while Auto is active (a manual pin disables all automation): ≥2 buffering edges within 60 s → step ONE rung down from the active one via ReloadKeepingPosition. Then a 90 s cooldown, at most 2 downshifts per video, never below the lowest rung, no upshifts. A downshift also updates the rung used by subsequent Auto resolves for this video.
  • Subtitles, audio-track choice, pause state and position survive the reload (existing machinery); the green buffered ranges show the refetch honestly.

4 · Visibility & testing

  • Rate in the quality menu: a muted footer line in BOTH quality flyouts (header and transport bar), e.g. "Measured: 42 Mbit/s" — refreshed when the flyout opens; "Simulated: 5 Mbit/s" while the override key is set. Out of the way in daily use, one click away in tests.
  • Decision log (sync.log, permanent): every Auto resolve logs auto: rate=42Mbps screen=1440 → 1440p; every downshift logs downshift: 2 stalls in 38s → 720p; a suppressed downshift (cooldown/cap) logs once why.
  • Manual gate: with simulatedBandwidthMbps at 4 / 25 / 200 the Auto pick lands on the expected rungs (log + label); with a NetLimiter/clumsy cap on the process, a high rung stalls and downshifts within the rules; cooldown and per-video cap respected; manual pin stops the automation; the flyout footer shows the live rate.

5 · Unchanged / out of scope

  • Manual quality picks, the subtitle/audio reload threading, buffered-range markers, previews.
  • Out of scope: continuous ABR, upshifting, per-network profiles, a settings UI.

6 · Testing (automated)

  • Core TDD: the rate-constrained pick (both constraints, unknown rate, bits/bytes conversion pinned, safety factor), raw-input-rate parsing, EMA smoothing (pure helper), the downshift decision logic (edges-in-window, cooldown, cap, lowest-rung floor) extracted as a pure, clock-injected Core class.
  • Settings round-trips for the two new keys follow the store's existing hostile-file discipline.
